# 0248. Every reason the Kernel gives is listed in one of two sets

Status: accepted. The Solution Architect's ruling of 2026-11-21, relayed by the Factory Builder, on the readability lane's plan, item 4, for "kernel: no list names every reason a Decision can carry" (issue 1105). This lands before item 4's own record, 0247, whose map from each reason to a plain sentence is held to `REASONS`.

## Decision

| What | Decided | Because |
|---|---|---|
| The two sets | `kernel/terms.py` holds two frozen sets: `REASONS`, every reason a pull request's Decision, decision/1, can carry; and `ISSUE_REASONS`, every reason only an issue's Decision, issue-decision/1, carries. They are apart. No rule reads them: they are data, and `tools/check_purity.py` is unchanged | A reason was written only where it was made, about a hundred of them across four modules. Nothing caught a typo in a reason or a reason that nothing gives any more. And a map from each reason to a sentence had nothing to be held to |
| A reason both kinds give | It is in `REASONS`. The set a reason is in follows from the schema the call that gives it returns: what the brakes, `run_deferral` or a reading both kinds share give a pull request's Decision is a pull request's reason, though an issue's Decision gives it too | The sentences of item 4 are for the factory check on a pull request. An issue's own reasons have no check to show |
| What the test reads | `tests/test_reasons.py` reads the Kernel's source. A reason is written as a Decision's or a reading's `"reason"`, as the argument `reason`, by name or by place, to any Kernel function with that parameter, as a value given the name `reason`, as a literal compared with a reason or with a table of reasons (`NEEDS_YOU_REASONS`, `QUIET_REASONS`, `SCOPE_DEFERRALS`, `GATE_REASONS`, `QUEUE_WAITS`, and `reading`'s `past`), and as a member of such a table. Each must be a literal, a choice between two literals, or a reason read on: the name `reason`, or a Decision's or a reading's `"reason"`. Anything else cannot be read, and fails. A literal in `kernel/issue.py` is in either set; in any other module it is in `REASONS` | So no reason is left out because it reaches a Decision through a helper, a table or a comparison. A comparison with a reason that is not in a set never matches, and is as wrong as a Decision that gives one |
| The three computed reasons | Three places put a reason they are handed into a Decision or a reading, rather than write one: `_decision` in `kernel/decide.py`, and `out` and `_decision` in `kernel/issue.py`. The test follows the parameter `reason` of these, and of every function that hands one on, to every call in the Kernel, so it reads the whole of what each can be given. `kernel/brakes.py` calls the `_decision` it is handed, and is read the same way | A reason made in one place and passed through others is read where it is made |
| The binding hold | The test records every Decision that the Kernel's boundary suites, `tests/test_decide.py` and `tests/test_issue.py`, and both simulated worlds on their fixed seeds give. A pull request's reason is in `REASONS`; an issue's is in either set | The source test reads what is written; this one reads what the Kernel gives |
| No dead member | Each member of `REASONS` is given by a pull request's Decision in that record, and each member of `ISSUE_REASONS` by an issue's. Each is also written somewhere in the Kernel. A reason only a new rule gives comes with that rule's boundary case in one of the two suites, which gives it | A list that can grow members nothing gives says less than it seems to |
| What stays | `schemas/decision.schema.json` and `schemas/issue-decision.schema.json` keep the reason's pattern, `^[a-z_]+$`, with no list of values | The schema is the contract with the ports. The sets are the Kernel's own, and change with it |

## Why

The readability plan's item 4 puts a plain sentence beside each reason on the factory check, and the Solution Architect ruled that the sentences be held to a list of reasons in the Kernel, in a change of its own before item 4. The source test reads every way the Kernel writes a reason today, and fails on any other way, so a new way has to be taught to it. The record of real Decisions holds each set to what the Kernel gives. The two suites gave every member when this record was written. Three issue reasons, `author_unreadable`, `content_unreadable` and `type_unreadable`, had been tested only through `derive`, so their tests now take the Decision too.

## Boundaries

| Boundary | Check |
|---|---|
| Every reason the Kernel writes or compares, by any way the test reads, is in its set, and anything else cannot be read and fails. Each member is written somewhere in the Kernel. Each way of writing a reason is read, and a call or an expression in a reason's place is refused | `tests/test_reasons.py::test_every_reason_the_kernel_writes_is_in_its_set`, `tests/test_reasons.py::test_a_reason_written_any_way_is_read`, `kernel/terms.py`, mutant: "a literal reason in the Kernel is in neither set", mutant: "a pull request's reason is listed as an issue's alone" |
| The two sets are frozen sets, apart, and hold codes | `tests/test_reasons.py::test_the_two_sets_are_apart_and_each_is_codes`, `kernel/terms.py`, mutant: "the two sets share a reason" |
| Every Decision the suites and the seeds give has its reason in its kind's set, and each member is given by a Decision of its kind. An unreadable author, content or type defers an issue's Decision with its own reason | `tests/test_reasons.py::test_every_decision_carries_a_reason_of_its_own_set`, `tests/test_reasons.py::test_every_member_is_given_by_a_decision_of_its_kind`, `tests/test_issue.py::test_an_unreadable_author_defers`, `tests/test_issue.py::test_unreadable_content_defers`, `tests/test_issue.py::test_an_unreadable_type_defers`, `kernel/terms.py`, mutant: "a member is dropped from REASONS", mutant: "a member nothing produces is added", mutant: "an issue-only reason is listed as a pull-request one" |
