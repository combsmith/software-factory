# 0193. The Maintainer's continue or stop is recorded in a file, and a decision counts only in his name

Status: accepted. Working ruling 7 on "The ruleset switch: main requires the machine user's approval of the most recent commit, and slice 1's event is recorded" (issue 198), which stands until the Maintainer overturns it.

## Decision

| What | Decided | Because |
|---|---|---|
| The record | `release/decision-points.json`, of schema `decision-points/1`, holds one entry for each decision point of the design's section "Stopping". An entry names its Release, what the Maintainer was shown beforehand, and his decision, its day and his login. The first, `first_release_on_next`, is recorded now; the first Consumer repo served is slice 3's to add, with its own measures | The design says the record is a file in the Factory repo, and gives it no form |
| Which Release, and which moment | The Release is the one that reviewed slice 1's event, as `release/fixtures/event.slice-1.json` records it. What is counted merged before that event merged | The design names the first Release on `next` that serves the Factory's repo, and no moment. The event is the merge slice 1 records as one that Release's approval let through |
| What he is shown | The pull requests merged into `main` before the event; how many of them he authored, and so read and merged himself; and how many changed a path `config/hooks.json` protects, as that list stood on the day of the read. They stand beside the design's starting figure of 40. Every merged pull request counts. The counts are read on the event's day or after. A day is a day in UTC, and one of the calendar | The design asks for the count against the figure, and for the share a protected path explains. It gives no start date and no rule for another author's |
| Who decides | `decided`, `day` and `by` are the Maintainer's to write, the three together or none. A decision counts only where `by` is the login `config/hooks.json` names him by, in any case of its letters; where that file names nobody, nobody's counts. The day is the day the counts were read, or later. A session fills in what he is shown and leaves the three empty | The decision is his by the design, and he makes it on what he was shown |
| What passing the figure does | Nothing. No check reads the count against the figure | The design: passing 40 stops nothing; it is the signal to shrink the slice or to fall back on the proof of concept's reviewer |
| What is not held | Whose hand wrote the three. The check holds the name written, and every commit names the Maintainer (0005), so nothing in the repo tells his edit from a session's, and the Factory may approve either. A session leaves the three empty by this row alone, until a change to the file is one only a person's approval passes. Nor are the counts held: they are read once, from GitHub through his login and from the repo's history | `verify` reads the commit alone, and neither GitHub nor the history is in it. Making the file a path a person must approve is a change to `config/hooks.json`, and its own decision |

## Why

The design gives the Maintainer two moments to decide whether the Factory goes on, and says the record is a file. The first moment came on 2026-11-11, when a Release on `next` reviewed a pull request to this repo and the machine user's approval let it merge. A decision that matters this much should be read from one place and show what he saw when he made it. A file a check holds gives both; it does not yet prove the hand, and the record says so instead of claiming it.
